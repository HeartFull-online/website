How to Stay Safe on Dating Apps

48% of dating app users report experiencing unwanted behavior. 88% of apps fail basic privacy standards. Here's how to protect yourself — regardless of which app you use.

Spotting fake profiles

Fake profiles are on every platform. Here's how to identify them:

Red flags in photos

  • Only 1-2 photos — real people share multiple angles and settings
  • All photos look professional — stock photo or model quality without any candid shots
  • Reverse image search — right-click and search Google Images; if the photo appears on other sites, it's stolen
  • No full-body photos or no photos with friends — bots rarely have social context

Red flags in behavior

  • Messages within seconds of matching — especially long, detailed messages. Real people take time.
  • Moves to WhatsApp/Signal immediately — scammers want to leave the app where they can't be reported
  • Asks for money — any financial request is a scam. No exceptions.
  • Refuses video calls — if someone can't do a quick video chat, they're likely not who they claim to be
  • "Investment opportunities" — crypto and investment pitches from matches are always scams

Protecting your data

Dating apps collect an average of 16 data points per user, including location, photos, messages, and behavioral patterns. 88% fail basic privacy standards.

What you can control

  • Don't link social media — avoid connecting Instagram or Facebook; it gives apps (and matches) access to far more personal info
  • Use a separate email — create a dedicated email for dating apps, not your primary one
  • Limit location precision — use "approximate" location if available; don't share your exact neighborhood
  • Review app permissions — deny access to contacts, call logs, and continuous location tracking
  • Don't share your workplace — job title is fine; specific company name + location makes you findable
Grindr users: The app has been fined 5.8M EUR for sharing user data (including HIV status) with advertisers. Review your privacy settings carefully and consider what health information you share on any platform.

First date safety

Before meeting

  • Video call first — confirms they're real and gives you a feel for the person before committing to a meeting
  • Tell a friend — share who you're meeting, where, and when. Set up a check-in time.
  • Google them — a quick search can reveal public information. It's not stalking; it's due diligence.

During the date

  • Meet in public — always. Coffee shops, restaurants, busy parks. Never a private location for a first meeting.
  • Arrange your own transport — don't rely on your date for getting home. Don't share your home address.
  • Watch your drink — don't leave drinks unattended. Accept drinks only from the bartender/server.
  • Trust your instincts — if something feels wrong, leave. You don't owe anyone your time or an explanation.

After the date

  • Text your friend that you're home safe
  • Don't share your home address until you've met multiple times and feel comfortable
  • Block and report if anything felt unsafe — don't second-guess yourself

Dealing with harassment

If someone harasses you on a dating app:

  1. Screenshot everything before blocking — evidence may be needed for reports
  2. Block immediately — don't engage or try to reason with harassers
  3. Report to the app — use the in-app reporting feature. It creates a record even if action feels slow.
  4. Report to police if threats are made — online threats are criminal in most jurisdictions
  5. You're not alone — 48% of users experience unwanted behavior. It's the platform's failure, not yours.

Protecting your mental health

Research shows dating apps can increase anxiety, depression, and lower self-esteem. Some practical strategies:

  • Set time limits — 15-20 minutes per day is enough. Endless swiping makes everything worse.
  • Don't take rejection personally — most "rejections" on apps are people who never saw your profile, not judgments of your worth
  • Take breaks — if the apps are making you feel worse about yourself, delete them for a while. They'll still be there.
  • Remember the business model — apps are designed to keep you engaged, not to make you happy. Your feelings of inadequacy may be by design, not a reflection of reality.
The best dating app safety feature is one no app offers: aligning the platform's incentives with your wellbeing. Until that changes, protect yourself with the tools you have.
