1. Global market size & growth

The global online dating market was valued at $6.2 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $17.6 billion by 2033, growing at a CAGR of 7.83% (Statista, Grand View Research). Growth is primarily driven by expansion in Asia-Pacific and Latin America, while mature markets like the US and Western Europe are showing signs of saturation.

Despite projected growth, the industry faces headwinds: declining user satisfaction, regulatory scrutiny over deceptive design patterns, and a growing cultural backlash against app-based dating — especially among Gen Z.

3. Market share & concentration

Two companies — Match Group and Bumble Inc — control approximately 70% of the Western dating app market. This level of concentration is unusual in consumer tech and has implications for pricing power, innovation, and user outcomes.

Match Group (Tinder + Hinge + OkCupid + Match + POF + others) = ~50%. Bumble Inc (Bumble + Badoo) = ~19%. Two companies = ~70% of the market.

In specific countries, concentration is even higher. In the United States, Tinder holds 42% of revenue share. In Japan, local apps like Pairs and Tapple compete with Tinder. In South Korea, domestic apps dominate entirely.

5. User demographics

Gender split

The gender imbalance on dating apps is one of their most consequential features. On Tinder, approximately 75% of users are male and 25% female. Bumble skews slightly less, at roughly 67/33. Hinge is similar to Bumble. This imbalance drives vastly different experiences by gender and fuels the monetization model — men pay more because competition is fiercer.

9. Fertility rate correlation

As dating app revenue grew from $47 million (2015) to over $6 billion (2025), fertility rates declined across every major market. While correlation is not causation, the pattern is consistent and raises important questions about whether the primary way people now meet is optimized for connection or for revenue.

Every country where dating apps achieved mass adoption has seen fertility decline. The US total fertility rate dropped from 2.12 (above replacement) to 1.53 (well below) over the same period that dating apps became the dominant way couples meet. 10. Methodology & sources

Market share is calculated from revenue data. Download figures are from Sensor Tower and app store analytics.
