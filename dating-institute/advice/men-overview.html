Dating Advice for Men

Honest, research-backed guidance. No pickup lines, no manipulation tactics, no "sigma male" nonsense. Just practical help for men who want to build genuine connections.

Men swipe right on an average of 46 profiles per day. Women swipe right on 6. The math alone creates a wildly different experience. Men face a numbers game where 95 out of every 100 swipes go nowhere. Women face an overwhelming flood of attention, most of it low-effort.

This isn't fair to either side. But understanding it is the first step toward doing something useful about it.

The 8x gap in match rates between men and women is a structural feature of dating apps, not a reflection of your worth. It's driven by the gender ratio (3:1 male-to-female on most apps) and different swiping behaviors.

What doesn't work

A lot of advice for men falls into two equally useless categories: pickup artist manipulation, and vague platitudes like "just be yourself." Neither respects your intelligence or your situation.

Mass-swiping doesn't work. Dating app algorithms penalize indiscriminate right-swiping. Tinder's ELO system and Hinge's algorithm both track your selectivity. Swiping right on everyone signals desperation to the algorithm, which buries your profile. You're literally making yourself less visible by trying harder.

Pickup tactics don't work. Negging, scripted openers, manufactured "frames" — women have seen all of it. These tactics are designed to simulate confidence, but they actually communicate insecurity. The men who do well on dating apps aren't running scripts. They're being specific, attentive, and genuine.

Bitterness doesn't work. The frustration is real. But putting "tired of games" or "if you can't hold a conversation, swipe left" in your bio tells every woman you're already angry before you've met her. That's not attractive in any context.

What actually works: be more intentional

When your match rate is 5%, every detail matters. The men who succeed on dating apps aren't better-looking — they're more intentional. They send fewer, better likes. They write profiles that say something real. They send messages that reference something specific. They treat each interaction as a human encounter, not a numbers game.

That's what this guide series is about. Not tricks. Not hacks. Just doing the basics with genuine care and attention.

The bigger picture

We run a dating app ourselves, so we'll be honest: apps are one tool for meeting people, not the only one. If you're only using apps, you're competing in the most crowded, most imbalanced marketplace possible.

78% of dating app users report emotional exhaustion. If that includes you, the answer might not be a better profile — it might be a different approach entirely. Join a climbing gym. Take a cooking class. Volunteer. Go to a book club. These aren't cope; they're statistically better environments. The gender ratio is balanced, the pressure is lower, and people actually see you as a person before deciding whether to date you.

Apps can work. But they work best for people who aren't depending on them entirely.

The best dating advice anyone can give a man: build a life you're genuinely excited about. Then invite someone into it. Not because it's a "strategy" — because people who are engaged with their own lives are fundamentally more attractive to be around.
