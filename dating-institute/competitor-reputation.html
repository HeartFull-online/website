User Sentiment

What People Actually Think About Dating Apps

We researched user reviews, complaint forums, and media coverage for every major dating app. The pattern is striking: the same complaints repeat across every platform. Fake profiles, aggressive monetization, terrible support, and a system that profits from keeping people single.

Tinder

“The hookup slot machine”

The original swipe app, now synonymous with hookup culture and pay-to-win dating.

  • Swipe fatigue: 79% of Gen Z report being drained by endless swiping
  • Pay-to-play: Every useful feature behind a $25–$50/mo paywall
  • Bots everywhere: Fake profiles slip through; reporting feels pointless
  • Mental health: Studies link regular use to lower self-esteem
  • Popularized ghosting: No pressure to follow through on matches
Trustpilot: ~1.2 stars • Reviews

Bumble

“Tinder, but yellow”

Positioned as feminist but widely seen as the same swipe-and-pay experience with a different color.

  • $80/mo Premium+: One of the priciest dating subscriptions
  • Unauthorized charges: Reports of billing after cancellation
  • Scammers: “Every person is a scammer” per multiple reviewers
  • Random bans: Accounts banned without explanation, no refund
  • Brand decline: Health score dropped 6 points in 3 months after controversial ads
Trustpilot: ~1.3 stars • Reviews

Hinge

“Designed to delete your wallet”

The “respectable” dating app that hides its best profiles behind paywalls.

  • Standouts paywall: Best-liked profiles cost $4+ per rose — described as “insidious”
  • Lifetime bans: Banned permanently with no explanation, even after paying $99+
  • Template support: Appeals get copy-paste “you violated ToS” with no evidence
  • Safety score: Independent reviews rate safety at just 5.2/10
Trustpilot: ~1.5 stars • Reviews

OkCupid

“RIP the free dating site (2004–2018)”

Once the smart, quirky, free platform. Acquired by Match Group and stripped of everything that made it different.

  • Was free, now $55/mo: Premium costs more than what was free in 2012
  • 75% fake profiles: Users estimate 3 out of 4 profiles are fake
  • Misleading likes: Shows “19 likes!” but real number far fewer after paying
  • Lost identity: Now indistinguishable from Tinder/Bumble after Match Group homogenized it
Trustpilot: ~1.2 stars • Reviews

Grindr

“Now with a $500/mo AI tier”

Dominant LGBTQ+ app, increasingly criticized for aggressive monetization and privacy violations.

  • $500/mo EDGE tier: AI subscription widely mocked
  • “Enshittification”: Free experience deliberately degraded to push paid tiers
  • Privacy scandals: Fined 5.8M EUR; accused of sharing HIV status without consent
  • 350K fake accounts removed in 2025 — users say it's still full of bots
Trustpilot: ~1.4 stars • Reviews

Badoo

“The one nobody chose on purpose”

Budget app full of bots. Users end up here by accident or desperation.

  • Bots from minute one: Fake messages within 2–3 minutes of creating an empty profile
  • Billing nightmare: Charged $131 unauthorized just for downloading
  • Lifetime sub doesn't cover everything: Pay for lifetime, still get paywalled for chat
Trustpilot: ~1.5 stars • Sitejabber: 1.5 stars • Reviews

Match.com

“Where profiles never die”

The original dating site, now a bloated relic with zombie profiles from years ago.

  • Zombie profiles: Profiles from 2014 still showing up in searches
  • Opaque pricing: Diamond tier pricing deliberately hidden
  • 58% price hike on some plans in 2026
  • Feature-gated: Need Platinum for any practically useful features
Trustpilot: ~1.5 stars

eHarmony

“29 dimensions of emptying your wallet”

Long contracts, aggressive auto-renewal, and standard features locked behind premium.

  • No monthly option: Minimum 6-month commitment
  • Auto-renewal trap: Aggressively renews before users can cancel
  • Up to $66/mo: One of the priciest dating services
Trustpilot: ~1.4 stars

Plenty of Fish

“The Walmart of dating apps”

Massive volume, terrible quality. Called “the trailer park of online dating” by its own users.

  • Spam inbox: Overwhelming low-effort and spam messages
  • Abandoned profiles: Higher proportion of fakes than paid services
  • Confusing pricing: Three tiers, three lengths, plus a la carte — no clear guide
Trustpilot: ~1.3 stars • Reviews

Coffee Meets Bagel

“One bagel a day, none that reply”

Promises quality over quantity. Delivers bugs, absent users, and upsells.

  • Buggy: Constantly freezes, photos won't load for 5+ minutes
  • Empty promises: Daily match timer resets with no new matches
  • “Blatant cash grab”: Pushed to buy premium for matches that don't materialize
Trustpilot: ~1.6 stars • Reviews

Happn

“Stalking, but make it romantic”

Shows people you've crossed paths with. Unique concept, but raises privacy concerns and barely works outside walkable cities.

  • Privacy nightmare: GPS tracking exposes daily routines
  • Fake messages: Free users flooded with computer-generated messages
  • Tiny user pool: “Lucky to get 10 profiles” outside major cities
Trustpilot: ~2.0 stars • Reviews

The League

“The velvet rope of loneliness”

Most openly elitist dating app. LinkedIn verification, waitlists, up to $2,500/mo.

  • $2,500/mo VIP: Most expensive dating app in existence
  • Ethnicity filters: Users can filter by race — called racist
  • Waitlist as feature: Artificial scarcity; paying to skip it
Trustpilot: ~1.8 stars • Reviews

Tantan

“Tinder's Asian twin”

China's Tinder clone. 300M+ users but estimated 95% are bots or scammers.

  • 95% fake profiles: Scammers, catfish, deepfake videos
  • Data privacy: Chinese-owned; collects location, chat logs, photos
  • Crypto scams: Profiles push users to fake investments
Trustpilot: ~1.3 stars • Reviews

Feeld

“Expensive for the open-minded”

Leading ENM/polyamory app. Strong values but buggy, gender-imbalanced, overpriced.

  • Buggy: Constant crashes, unhelpful support
  • 1:500 ratio: “Borderline unusable for straight men”
  • Identity crisis: Mainstream users diluting the community
Trustpilot: ~1.5 stars • Reviews

Common themes

Across all 14 apps, the same complaints repeat: fake profiles, aggressive monetization, terrible customer support, unauthorized charges, and a system that profits from keeping users single. The average Trustpilot rating across all 14 is approximately 1.5 stars.
