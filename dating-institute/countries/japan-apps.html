Dating App Landscape: Japan

Japan's dating app market is a paradox. It generates 78.8 billion yen (~$530 million) in revenue, yet dating apps represent only 0.08% of all app downloads in the country — the lowest share among major economies. The apps that do succeed are overwhelmingly relationship-focused, reflecting a culture where marriage is the expected outcome of dating.

Market dynamics

Japan's dating app landscape is the inverse of the Western model. In the US, Tinder dominates through volume and casual swiping. In Japan, Pairs dominates through commitment. The app charges men a monthly subscription (~$35/month) while women use it free, reflecting traditional Japanese dating norms where men are expected to pay. Pairs claims to be responsible for roughly 10% of marriages in Japan.

Tapple matches users around shared hobbies and interests, generating 250,000 daily matches. Omiai — whose name literally means "formal matchmaking" — reports that 44% of its users are explicitly seeking marriage. with uses psychological profiling based on personality assessments. The through-line is clear: Japanese dating apps succeed when they signal seriousness and intentionality.

Despite this relationship-first approach, the apps have not reversed Japan's fertility decline. Marriages hit a record low of 505,656 in 2024. The apps may help some people find partners, but they operate within a broader system — overwork culture, economic precarity, housing costs — that makes family formation structurally difficult regardless of how people meet.

What this tells us

Japan proves that even marriage-oriented dating apps cannot fix a broken system. Pairs is arguably the most relationship-aligned major dating app in the world — it claims 10% of marriages, it charges for seriousness, it explicitly targets long-term commitment. Yet Japan's fertility rate continues to fall (1.15 in 2024), and marriages keep hitting new lows.

For HeartFull, the lesson is that app design alone is necessary but not sufficient. Aligning incentives toward relationships (as Pairs does) is better than optimizing for engagement (as Tinder does). But the broader ecosystem — work culture, economic conditions, social norms — must also change. HeartFull's model of disconnecting payments from connections is a step in the right direction, but humility about what any single app can achieve is warranted.
