Dating App Safety: United States

The US is the world's largest dating app market — and also its most dangerous by dollar losses. Americans lost $1.3 billion to romance scams in 2022 alone, with a median loss of $4,400 per victim. Yet federal regulation remains minimal, and platforms enjoy broad legal immunity under Section 230.

Section 230: the platform shield

Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act grants dating platforms near-total immunity from liability for user-generated content. If a user creates a fake profile and defrauds or harms someone, the platform generally cannot be sued. Courts have consistently upheld this protection — including a case where a Grindr user was relentlessly harassed via a fake profile created by an ex-partner. The victim's claims for product liability and design defect were dismissed under Section 230.

The 2018 FOSTA-SESTA amendment carved out an exception for sex trafficking, but no equivalent exception exists for romance fraud, catfishing, or assault facilitated through dating apps. This means platforms face minimal legal incentive to invest in user safety beyond what is needed for brand reputation.

State-level age verification

With no federal age-verification mandate for dating apps, a patchwork of state laws has emerged. Several states — including Louisiana, Virginia, Texas, and Utah — have passed age verification requirements for online platforms, though most target pornography rather than dating apps specifically. New Jersey's Internet Dating Safety Act is one of the few laws directly addressing dating platforms, requiring disclosures about whether background checks are performed.

The Surgeon General's loneliness advisory

In May 2023, U.S. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy declared loneliness a public health epidemic, noting that lacking social connection is as harmful as smoking 15 cigarettes a day. The advisory specifically called for evaluating digital environments to ensure they do not interfere with meaningful human connection — a pointed reference to the engagement-maximizing design of dating apps. The advisory added national urgency to the question of whether swipe-based apps are part of the solution or part of the problem.

Romance scam landscape

The FTC received nearly 70,000 romance scam reports in 2022, with losses totaling $1.3 billion. In 2023, reported losses were $1.14 billion across 64,000 reports. Cryptocurrency and bank wire transfers accounted for over 60% of losses, while gift cards were the most frequently reported payment method (24% of cases). Social media — not dating apps alone — was the #1 contact method for scammers, with $2.7 billion lost to social media fraud from January 2021 to June 2023.

Tips for US dating app users

Verify independently. Reverse-image-search profile photos before meeting. Never send money to someone you have not met in person — no matter the story. Meet in public for first dates and tell a friend your plans. Use the app's messaging as long as possible before moving to personal channels. Report suspicious profiles — the FTC accepts complaints at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
