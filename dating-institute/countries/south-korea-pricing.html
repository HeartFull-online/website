Dating App Prices in South Korea (2026)

South Korea's dating app market is dominated by domestic apps that emphasize credential gating and appearance screening — features that Western apps lack. Many Korean apps require identity verification and some, like Amanda, require passing an attractiveness vote before you can even join. Premium features often revolve around tier upgrades and visibility boosts rather than simple unlimited likes.

Credential gating costs extra

Korean dating apps often use credential verification as a premium feature or access gate. On Amanda, users must pass an appearance vote by existing members before gaining entry — the process itself is free, but being rejected means you cannot use the app at all. GLAM separates users into tiers based on profile quality, and moving up tiers (to access more desirable matches) requires premium features or profile investment. Some apps offer university and employer verification badges that improve match rates but may cost extra or require documentation.

How Korea compares to US prices

Korean domestic apps are notably cheaper than US apps. Noondate's entry plan at ₩9,900 (~$7/month) undercuts nearly every US subscription. Even the most expensive Korean app plans top out around ₩55,000 (~$40), while US apps like Tinder Select charge $499/month. Tinder and Bumble's Korean prices are also lower than their US equivalents — Tinder Plus is roughly half the US price in Korea. The overall cost of dating app subscriptions in Korea runs 30–50% below US levels.

iOS vs. Android and payment methods

As in most markets, subscribing through iOS costs more due to Apple's commission. Korean users can often save by paying through the app's website or via Korean payment processors (KakaoPay, Toss, Naver Pay). Some apps offer discounts for credit card payment over app store billing. Android prices via Google Play tend to fall between iOS and web pricing.

Tips for getting the best deal

1. Use domestic apps. Amanda, Noondate, and GLAM are cheaper and have larger Korean user bases than Tinder or Bumble.
2. Pay via KakaoPay or web. Avoid the App Store markup by subscribing through the app's website.
3. GLAM is free-first. GLAM's core messaging and video calls are free, making it the best budget option.
4. Invest in your profile. On tier-based apps like GLAM, a strong profile photo and detailed bio can get you into higher tiers for free — no premium needed.
5. Watch for promotions. Korean apps frequently offer seasonal discounts, especially around holidays like Pepero Day (November 11) and Valentine's Day.
