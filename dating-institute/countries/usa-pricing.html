Dating App Prices in USA (2026)

Americans spend over $3.5 billion per year on dating apps. Prices vary wildly — from $0 to $499/month — and are increasingly dynamic, meaning two users on the same app may see completely different prices based on age, location, and platform.

Dynamic and age-based pricing

Most US dating apps use dynamic pricing algorithms. Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge all adjust prices based on factors including age, location, and engagement patterns. Users under 28 typically see prices 20–40% lower than older users. A 2024 Tinder lawsuit settlement confirmed the company had been charging users over 30 significantly more for identical features. Bumble and OkCupid have adopted similar age-tiered pricing, though they are less transparent about it.

iOS vs. Android pricing

Apple's 30% App Store commission is often passed to users. On many apps, subscribing via iOS costs 15–30% more than subscribing through the web or Google Play. Tinder Gold, for example, may show $39.99 on iOS and $29.99 on the web. The cheapest option is almost always to subscribe through the app's own website using a desktop browser.

Tips for getting the best deal

1. Subscribe via the web. Skip the App Store markup by going directly to the app's website on a desktop browser.
2. Choose longer plans. 6- and 12-month plans can be 50–70% cheaper per month than monthly billing.
3. Wait for promotions. Most apps run sales around Valentine's Day, New Year, and Black Friday — discounts of 30–50% are common.
4. Use the free tier strategically. Hinge's 8 free likes/day are enough for selective daters. Bumble's core messaging is free. You do not need premium to get dates.
5. Check for VPN pricing. Some users report lower prices when their billing region differs, though apps are increasingly closing this loophole.
