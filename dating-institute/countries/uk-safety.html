Dating App Safety: United Kingdom

The UK has become one of the most actively regulated dating markets in the world. The Online Safety Act 2023 places new legal obligations on dating platforms, Ofcom enforces age verification from July 2025, and romance fraud remains a serious threat — with British victims losing £106 million in 2024 alone.

The Online Safety Act 2023

The Online Safety Act, which received Royal Assent in October 2023, is the most significant piece of internet regulation in UK history. It directly impacts dating apps by requiring platforms to remove illegal content including child sexual abuse material, criminalizing fraudulent and scam advertisements, creating new criminal offences for cyberflashing (sending unsolicited nude images via dating apps), and mandating age verification for services hosting restricted content.

Ofcom, the UK media regulator, began enforcing the Act's age verification provisions from July 25, 2025. Dating apps must take "reasonable steps" to verify user ages, which can include government-issued ID checks, credit card verification, or third-party age verification services. This represents a sharp departure from the self-certification model most apps previously relied on.

Romance fraud: the numbers

The National Fraud Intelligence Bureau recorded 9,449 romance fraud reports in the 2024/25 financial year — a 9% increase year-over-year. Victims lost an estimated £106 million, with an average loss of £11,222 per person. The 50–59 age group suffered the highest total losses at £22.1 million. Men were slightly more likely to be victimized, but women lost larger sums on average due to longer manipulation periods.

The National Crime Agency classifies romance fraud as a priority threat, noting that reports remain at the elevated levels first seen during the pandemic. Increasingly sophisticated tactics — including social engineering and deepfake impersonation — are driving both the volume and severity of losses.

Ask for Angela

The UK pioneered one of the world's most recognized real-world dating safety schemes. Ask for Angela is a nationwide initiative where anyone feeling unsafe on a date can approach bar or pub staff and "ask for Angela" — a code phrase indicating they need help. Staff are trained to discreetly assist by reuniting the person with a friend, calling a taxi, or contacting police.

Originally developed by Lincolnshire County Council and adopted by the Metropolitan Police in 2016, the scheme is named in memory of Angela Crompton, who was killed by her husband in 2012. It has since been replicated internationally, including in the United States and Australia, and is displayed in thousands of venues across the UK.

GDPR and data rights

UK dating app users benefit from strong data protection rights under UK GDPR (retained from EU law). Users have the right to request all personal data a dating app holds about them, demand deletion of their data, withdraw consent for data processing, and receive their data in a portable format. These rights are particularly important given that dating apps collect some of the most sensitive personal information of any consumer service — including sexual orientation, relationship preferences, location history, and private messages.

Tips for UK dating app users

Know your code words. Remember "Ask for Angela" at any pub, bar, or restaurant. Report fraud early — contact Action Fraud (0300 123 2040) or report online at actionfraud.police.uk. Exercise your data rights — request your data from any dating app under UK GDPR. Verify before you meet — video call first and tell a friend your plans. Never send money to someone you have only met online.
