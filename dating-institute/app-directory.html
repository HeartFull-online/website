Every Dating App, Assessed

17 dating apps. Same business model. Same complaints. We researched each one — pricing, reputation, what makes them different (or not), and what they get wrong.

Average Trustpilot rating across all 17: approximately 1.5 stars. Every app listed here paywalls connections. HeartFull is the only one that doesn't.

● Tinder

“The hookup slot machine”
$25–50/moTrustpilot ~1.2★63M downloads/yrMatch Group

The original swipe app that defined modern dating. Now synonymous with hookup culture, swipe fatigue, and pay-to-win. 79% of Gen Z report being drained by the experience.

Pros
  • Largest user base globally
  • Instant matches possible
  • Universal brand recognition
Cons
  • Swipe fatigue — endless loop
  • Pay-to-win ($50/mo Platinum)
  • Popularized ghosting
  • Mental health impact documented
Owned by Match Group ($3.5B/yr revenue). The app that proved dating could be monetized at scale — at the cost of user satisfaction.

● Bumble

“Tinder, but yellow”
$17–80/moTrustpilot ~1.3★29M downloads/yrBumble Inc

Positioned as the feminist dating app where women message first. In practice, the same swipe-and-pay experience with a different color. Premium+ at $80/mo is one of the most expensive subscriptions in dating.

Pros
  • Women-first messaging reduces spam
  • BFF and Bizz modes
  • Strong brand recognition
Cons
  • $80/mo Premium+ tier
  • Unauthorized charges reported
  • Random bans without explanation
  • Brand health declining (6-point drop)
Bumble Inc ($966M revenue). The "women message first" gimmick doesn't change the fundamental economics — connections are still paywalled.

● Hinge

“Designed to delete your wallet”
$30–50/moTrustpilot ~1.5★25% YoY growthMatch Group

The "respectable" dating app with prompt-based profiles. Hides its best profiles behind paywalls (roses cost $3.99 each). "Designed to be deleted" — after emptying your wallet.

Pros
  • Prompt-based profiles show personality
  • Less superficial than pure swiping
  • Growing fastest of the big three
Cons
  • Best profiles behind roses paywall
  • Lifetime bans with no explanation
  • Template support responses
  • Safety score: 5.2/10
Match Group's fastest-growing property. Better product design than Tinder, same exploitative business model underneath.

● Grindr

“Now with a $500/mo AI tier”
$20–500/moTrustpilot ~1.4★12M downloads/yrGrindr Inc

Dominant LGBTQ+ app with a grid-based interface. Increasingly aggressive monetization including a $500/mo "EDGE" AI tier. Fined 5.8M EUR for sharing HIV status with advertisers.

Pros
  • Largest LGBTQ+ user base
  • Location-based proximity
  • No real alternative for many users
Cons
  • $500/mo AI tier (EDGE)
  • Privacy scandals (HIV data shared)
  • "Enshittification" of free tier
  • 350K fake accounts removed in 2025
Users feel trapped because there's no alternative. The definition of a monopoly exploiting its position.

● OkCupid

“RIP the free dating site (2004–2018)”
$35–55/moTrustpilot ~1.2★70M usersMatch Group

Once the smart, quirky, genuinely free dating platform. Acquired by Match Group and stripped of everything that made it different. Now indistinguishable from Tinder with a match percentage badge.

Pros
  • Detailed compatibility questions
  • Match percentage system
  • More inclusive identity options
Cons
  • Was free, now $55/mo premium
  • ~75% fake profiles reported
  • Misleading "likes" counter
  • Lost its identity post-acquisition
The cautionary tale of what happens when Match Group acquires an independent platform. Everything good was stripped for monetization.

● Match.com

“Where profiles never die”
$19–46/moTrustpilot ~1.5★Est. 1995Match Group

The original dating site, now a bloated relic. Profiles from years ago still show up in searches. Opaque pricing with some plans up 58% in 2026.

Pros
  • Long track record
  • Older, more relationship-focused users
Cons
  • Zombie profiles from 2014+
  • 58% price hikes in 2026
  • Opaque Diamond tier pricing
  • Upfront lump-sum payment required
The company that became Match Group. Now the parent of the entire problem — $3.5B/yr from paywalling human connection.

● eHarmony

“29 dimensions of emptying your wallet”
$19–66/moTrustpilot ~1.4★6-month minimum

Markets itself as the serious relationship app with a compatibility algorithm. The 29-dimension quiz is the hook; the 6-month minimum commitment is the trap. Aggressive auto-renewal.

Pros
  • Detailed compatibility matching
  • More relationship-focused users
  • Strong brand for 35+ demographic
Cons
  • No monthly option (6-month min)
  • Aggressive auto-renewal
  • Up to $66/mo
  • Standard features locked behind premium
Long contracts + auto-renewal = users paying for months after they've given up. The subscription trap dressed as science.

● Plenty of Fish

“The Walmart of dating apps”
$11–21/moTrustpilot ~1.3★150M registeredMatch Group

Called "the Walmart of dating" and "the trailer park of online dating" by its own users. Massive volume, terrible quality. Inbox flooded with spam.

Pros
  • Low barrier to entry
  • Large user base
  • Some free messaging
Cons
  • Spam inbox overwhelming
  • High proportion of fake/abandoned profiles
  • Confusing 3-tier pricing
  • Outdated interface

● Coffee Meets Bagel

“One bagel a day, none that reply”
$9–35/moTrustpilot ~1.6★Quality over quantity

Promises curated matches — one "bagel" per day. In practice: the app freezes constantly, matches rarely respond, and premium is described as a "blatant cash grab."

Pros
  • Less overwhelming than swipe apps
  • Curated daily matches
  • Shared interests highlighted
Cons
  • Buggy (crashes, photos won't load)
  • Empty promises (no new matches)
  • Many absentee/inactive users
  • Premium upsells for basic features
Good idea, poor execution. The "quality over quantity" promise falls apart when the quality is bugs and absent users.

● Happn

“Stalking, but make it romantic”
$10–25/moTrustpilot ~2.0★70M users

Shows people you've physically crossed paths with. Unique concept, but raises serious privacy concerns — researchers flagged that GPS data can expose daily routines. Barely works outside walkable cities.

Pros
  • Unique "missed connections" model
  • Feels more organic than swiping
  • Good in dense urban areas
Cons
  • Privacy nightmare (GPS tracking)
  • Computer-generated fake messages
  • Tiny user pool outside cities
  • "Stalker's Dream" reputation
Innovative concept ruined by privacy implications and geographic limitations. If you don't walk to work, it's useless.

● The League

“The velvet rope of loneliness”
$300–2,500/moTrustpilot ~1.8★Invite-only

The most openly elitist dating app. LinkedIn verification, waitlists, and up to $2,500/mo for VIP. Criticized as classist and racist (ethnicity filters). Artificial scarcity as a feature.

Pros
  • Curated, career-oriented users
  • Weekly speed dating events
  • LinkedIn verification
Cons
  • $2,500/mo VIP tier
  • Ethnicity filters (called racist)
  • Waitlist as artificial scarcity
  • Poor matching despite elite claims
The logical extreme of pay-to-win dating. When you charge $2,500/mo, you're not selling connections — you're selling status anxiety.

Pros

“AI matchmaking that drains your wallet”
$8–30/moTrustpilot ~1.9★40M users
