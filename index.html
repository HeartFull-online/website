The real problem
nobody talks about

Your photos. Your stories. Their $4.5 billion.

Dating apps don't create any content. Every photo, every bio, every conversation — that's all you. The platforms are empty shells without their users. Yet Match Group alone made $3.5 billion last year. Bumble added another $966 million. They use your attractiveness, your personality, your effort as bait to sell subscriptions to others. You are the product. You just aren't getting paid.

Money buys advantage. That's the design.

Pay $50/mo and your profile gets boosted. Pay more and you see who liked you. Pay even more and your messages land at the top. Can't afford it? You get buried. The person who can pay $80/mo to Bumble has a measurably better chance of finding love than someone who can't. Your dating success — arguably one of the most important things in life — is determined by your income.

People become a commodity.

When you can buy access to people — buy the right to see them, message them, stand out to them — people become products with a price tag. “See who liked you for $39.99.” That's a human being behind that paywall. Their interest in you, monetized. Their attention, sold. This is what commodification looks like.

If this is how society meets, it affects all of society.

Dating apps are now the #1 way couples meet. Over a quarter of 2025 marriages started on one. It's how people find partners, form families, and build lives together. If that market is distorted by money — if who you meet, match, and mate with is influenced by who can afford a premium subscription — then the apps aren't just affecting individuals. They're shaping relationships, families, and communities at scale. And they're doing it in a way that advantages wealth over character.

Everyone ends up less happy.

The people who pay feel anxious — “am I paying enough? Should I upgrade?” The people who don't pay feel invisible and frustrated. And the platforms? They're incentivized to keep you single and swiping, because the moment you find love, you cancel your subscription. The entire system is designed to profit from loneliness. No wonder 79% of Gen Z report dating app fatigue.

This is why HeartFull exists. Not to build a better dating app — but to fix the broken incentive at the root. Disconnect money from connections entirely. Let character determine who you meet, not your credit card.

The Hearts Economy

Every dating app has an in-app currency — coins, boosts, super likes. The difference? They sell theirs for cash. Ours can only be earned through how you treat people. Hearts can never be purchased.

This is the cornerstone: payments are completely disconnected from connections. We'll monetize premium tools like AI and voice — never your ability to meet and talk to people.

Earn Hearts

  • ♥ Verify yourself — post a real-time selfie confirmed by AI. Proves you're real, earns you Hearts.
  • ♥ Complete your profile — answer personality prompts, add photos. The more you share, the more you earn.
  • ♥ Be responsive — reply to messages, show up to conversations. Consistency is rewarded.
  • ♥ Be kind — positive interactions earn Hearts. Ghosting, rudeness, and spam drain them.

Spend Hearts

  • ♥ Start a conversation — reaching out costs Hearts, so you think about what to say. No more “hey” spam.
  • ♥ Approach high-value users — messaging someone with many Hearts costs more. It protects popular users from inbox flooding.
  • ♥ Champion underdogs — newer members cost fewer Hearts to approach. Give someone a chance who hasn't been discovered yet.
  • ♥ Boost visibility — spend Hearts to get seen by more people. The same feature other apps charge $50/mo for.

How Hearts actually work

Hearts aren't a gimmick. They're a complete economy that replaces money with merit. Here's exactly how it works.

1

You start with 100 Hearts

Every new user gets 100 Hearts on sign-up. Enough to start conversations and explore, but not enough to spam. From this point, everything you do earns or costs Hearts.

2

Earn more by investing in your profile

Upload a photo? +20 Hearts. Post a verified selfie? Hearts. Answer personality prompts? Hearts. The more real and complete your profile, the more you earn. No shortcuts.

3

First message? 1 Heart in escrow

When you message someone for the first time, 1 Heart goes into escrow — held between you. If they reply, they claim it. If they don't, it stays reserved. This tiny cost proves genuine interest on both sides and eliminates copy-paste openers.

4

The cost to approach depends on the gap

This is the key insight. The cost to message someone scales with the difference between your Heart counts. It's not a flat fee — it's a ratio:

Scenario You Them Cost
You're both equal 100 100 1 Heart
You're reaching up 50 500 5 Hearts (10%)
You're well-established 1,000 100 10 Hearts (1%)

Reaching up costs more (10% of your Hearts). Reaching down costs less (1%). Equals pay just 1 Heart.

5

Hearts flow between people, not to us

When you spend Hearts to message someone, those Hearts go directly to them — not to HeartFull. We take nothing. The entire economy flows between users. We'll make money from premium tools like AI assistance, voice messages, and speech-to-text — never from limiting who you can meet or talk to. That's the line every other app crossed. We won't.
