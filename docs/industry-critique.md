# The Dating App Industry Critique

## The Core Problem

Every major dating app has the same fundamental flaw: **they tie money to connections.** Matching, messaging, and visibility are all gated by payment. This creates a system where your dating success is determined by your income, not your character.

## Users Are the Product

Dating apps don't create any content. Every photo, every bio, every conversation is user-generated. The platforms are empty shells without their users. Yet they extract billions:

| Company | 2025 Revenue | Content Created | Content Owned |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Match Group (Tinder, Hinge, OkCupid, Match, POF) | $3.5 billion | 0% | 0% |
| Bumble Inc (Bumble, Badoo) | $966 million | 0% | 0% |
| **Combined** | **$4.5 billion** | **0%** | **0%** |

The platforms use people's attractiveness, personality, and effort as bait to sell subscriptions to others. Users are the product. They just aren't getting paid.

## Money Buys Advantage

Pay-to-win mechanics create a two-tier dating market:

**Paying users get:**
- Unlimited swipes (vs ~25/day on free tier)
- See who liked them (hidden behind paywall)
- Profile boosts (appear more often to others)
- Super likes / roses (signal stronger interest)
- Priority placement in feeds
- Advanced filters (age, distance, education, etc.)

**Free users get:**
- Limited swipes
- Can't see who liked them
- Buried in the feed
- Basic filters only
- Slower match rates

The result: **someone who can afford $80/mo on Bumble Premium+ has a measurably better chance of finding a partner than someone who can't.** Dating success — arguably one of the most important outcomes in life — is determined by income.

## People Become Commodities

When you can buy access to people — buy the right to see them, message them, stand out to them — people become products with a price tag.

- "See who liked you for $39.99" → That's a human being behind that paywall
- "Boost your profile for $9.99" → Buy visibility among other humans
- "Send a rose for $3.99" → Pay to signal interest in a person

This is commodification. Human attention, interest, and connection are being packaged and sold.

## Societal Impact

This matters beyond individual experiences because dating apps are now the **primary way people meet:**

| Metric | Data | Source |
|--------|------|--------|
| Couples who met through dating apps | 50%+ of engaged couples surveyed (2025) | The Knot Survey |
| Marriages from dating apps | 27% of 2025 marriages | The Knot Real Weddings Study |
| Most common way couples meet | Online dating (#1 channel in 2026) | SSRS / Pew |
| Gen Z dating app fatigue | 79% report being drained | Multiple studies |

If dating apps are how most people find partners, form families, and build lives — and if that market is distorted by money — then the apps are shaping relationships, families, and communities at scale. They're doing it in a way that advantages wealth over character.

### The cascade:
1. Money determines visibility on dating apps
2. Visibility determines who matches with whom
3. Matches determine who dates, mates, and forms families
4. Families shape communities and society
5. Therefore: **money is influencing the formation of families and society through distorted dating markets**

## Everyone Ends Up Less Happy

- **Paying users** feel anxiety: "Am I paying enough? Should I upgrade?"
- **Free users** feel invisible and frustrated
- **The platforms** are incentivized to keep users single and swiping (happy couples cancel subscriptions)
- **79% of Gen Z** report dating app fatigue
- Studies link regular dating app use to lower self-esteem and worse body image

The entire system is designed to profit from loneliness. The longer you stay single, the longer you pay.
